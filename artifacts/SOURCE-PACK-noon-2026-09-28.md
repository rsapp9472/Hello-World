**SpaceX Starship reaches Earth orbit for the first time, then the flight is cut short.** Flight 14 lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 8:48am ET. After one upper-stage engine shut down and controllers briefly called off the orbit attempt, they reversed and inserted the ship — the first orbital Starship flight after a string of suborbital tests. It deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites (first orbital payload for the vehicle). By about 10:22am ET SpaceX scrapped the planned six orbits / ~10-hour flight and began a deorbit burn around 11:02am ET, aiming to splash down in roughly three hours, north of Hawaii rather than west of Chile. Reentry was underway by about 11:51am ET; splashdown itself was not confirmed inside this window. Matters for NASA’s lunar architecture, Starlink capacity, and a company that listed in June. — https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c65ymmpzev1mt — also Reuters (Joey Roulette) and AP (Marcia Dunn); Guardian writeup 10:44am ET: https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/sep/28/spacex-starship-rocket-orbits-earth-texas — `in_window: yes` — `new_step_change: yes`
