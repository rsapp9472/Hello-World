## Track A candidates

**U.S. consumer confidence fell hard in September.** Conference Board index dropped 6.7 points to 81.9 (from a revised 88.6 in August; the Aug. 25 release had printed 89.4), well under a ~89 consensus. Present Situation fell 7.9 points to 109.3 and current business-condition appraisals turned net negative for the first time since September 2024. Expectations fell 5.9 points to 63.6, a third straight monthly drop. Write-ins on prices and oil/gas “rose to new heights.” Average 12-month inflation expectations rose 0.3 point to 6.1% (median 5.1%); the share expecting higher interest rates jumped 5.2 points to 68.4%. The labor-market differential (plentiful minus hard to get) fell to +1.7. CNBC called 81.9 the lowest reading since 2014; the Board text itself does not use that phrase. Survey cutoff was Sept. 23, so it includes the September Fed hike and the fuel shock, not this morning’s tape. — 10:00am ET — https://www.conference-board.org/topics/consumer-confidence — wire: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-consumer-confidence-fell-in-september-302892867.html — CNBC 10:36am ET: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/consumer-optimism-slides-sharply-as-fears-escalate-over-rising-prices-and-jobs.html — `NEW`

**August job openings missed, without a layoff break.** BLS JOLTS (10:00am ET): openings fell 256,000 to 7.079 million (about 7.08 million), from an upwardly revised 7.335 million in July, versus forecasts near 7.2–7.23 million. Largest drops: professional and business services (−119,000), health care and social assistance (−115,000), manufacturing (−54,000), construction (−48,000), real estate (−45,000). Openings fell in the West (−243,000) and rose in the South (+71,000). Hires little changed around 5.2 million; separations unchanged around 5.1 million; quits unchanged around 3.1 million; layoffs essentially unchanged around 1.6 million (CNBC’s gloss was “hires edged higher” and “layoffs slipped”). A Yahoo live blog printed 7.097 million; the 256k decline and 7.08 million figure are the ones that match across CNBC and Trading Economics. BLS HTML was still serving the July release to automated fetches, so the August level is from those two secondary reads of the 10am print, not a direct BLS scrape. Matters for Friday’s payrolls and the October hike odds. — ~10:00am ET — https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/consumer-optimism-slides-sharply-as-fears-escalate-over-rising-prices-and-jobs.html — https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/job-offers — `NEW`
