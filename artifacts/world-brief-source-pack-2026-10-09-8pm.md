## Track A — World / US

### A1. Trump–Putin diesel deal; OFAC General License 135 — IN WINDOW

Trump said Friday he and Putin agreed Russia would put diesel back on the market to pull down prices before the Nov. 3 midterms: more than 300,000 metric tons immediately, 500,000 tons in November, 1 million tons after that, and a further amount (reported up to 3 million tons) conditional on damaged Russian refineries. Treasury then issued **Russia-related General License 135**, authorizing transactions related to the sale, delivery, offloading, and importation — including into the United States — of Russian-origin diesel through **12:01 a.m. EDT, April 7, 2027**. The license does not authorize debits to accounts of the Central Bank of Russia, the National Wealth Fund, or the Finance Ministry. Zelensky called easing sanctions a gift that funds the war; CNBC recorded congressional criticism that the waiver cuts against the Lindsey Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act signed about three weeks earlier. BBC: U.S. average diesel still about **$6.28/gal** (AAA), versus a late-September record near $6.53. Putin confirmed willingness to supply oil and products but did not restate the tonnage. What Russia gets in return was not specified.

**Afternoon delta:** the Truth Social announcement, the OFAC license (release page timestamped 19:47 UTC / 3:47pm ET), Zelensky’s response, and the Hill reaction all landed after noon ET. CNBC’s wrap is 7:02pm EDT.

### A2. Hurricane Isaias nearing the Florida Panhandle; Gulf oil shut-ins stepped up — IN WINDOW

At the **8:00pm ET close** (NHC intermediate advisory 13A, 7:00pm CDT), Isaias was **not yet ashore**. Center about 45 miles southeast of Pensacola, moving north at 18 mph, **110 mph**, pressure **967 mb**, landfall on the western Florida Panhandle expected within one to two hours. Hurricane conditions were already onshore (Gulf Breeze sustained 83 mph, gust 97 mph). Storm-surge warning Mouth of the Mississippi to the Suwannee; peak surge forecast **6–9 ft** from the AL/FL line to Grayton Beach; 4–8 inches of rain, local 15, with flash-flood and tornado risk east of the center into Saturday.

**Afternoon delta:** 1:00pm CDT it was still 120 mph and 145 miles south of Pensacola (959 mb). By 4:00pm CDT, 115 mph and 90 miles south, “conditions deteriorating quickly.” By 7:00pm CDT it had weakened to 110 mph and was inside an hour or two of landfall. AP’s early-evening copy still had it offshore and said the track should miss the biggest Gulf oil hubs, with refinery risk mainly from power loss.

**Shut-ins (official, data as of 11:00am CDT — noon ET, the window open):** Marine Minerals Administration (release on bsee.gov) estimated **1,458,814 bpd, 71.51%** of Gulf of America oil and **58.84%** of gas shut in; 129 platforms and 8 non-DP rigs evacuated. That is up from Thursday’s **62.89% / 1,282,879 bpd**.
